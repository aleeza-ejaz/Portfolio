## Overview
Portfolios are meant to convey a designer's voice. 


As a perfected body of work, they stand to document the thoughts and experiments that led to a finished product. In this way, they act as an extension of the artist.


In the Spring semester of 2026, I took a Web Design course offered by my university. We were taught the fundamentals of front-end development, along with the importance of establishing an online brand for creatives in this day and age. Before applying to internships, I challenged myself to put together portfolio website to serve as my platform on the internet.

## The Challenge
Designing and building a website that would best convey: "Hi, I'm Aleeza, and this is what I do." 

## Tech Stack {box}
+ **HTML5**:  structure, bringing in my voice
+ **CSS3**: stylization, sharing my vision 
+ **JS**: automation, building the details

## Details {box}
**18-year-old me inspired this project the most.**
In my senior year of high school, I joined the Yearbook club and got really involved in book's the design process. I ended up illustrating the cover, designing various original tailpieces, and having my art bring together the final aesthetic of the book. I reused some of the ideas I sketched from back then to inspire the color palette and animations you see on the home page, and the rest was designed from there.

## What I Learned
Working without a framework forced me to deeply understand **CSS layout fundamentals** — especially how stacking contexts, `position: sticky`, and `clamp()` interact.

I also hit cross-browser issues with `backdrop-filter` that taught me to test on Safari early.

## Outcome {outcome}
A fully responsive, accessible portfolio site with custom animations, zero dependencies, and a clear design system — built and shipped for my 2026 job search.
