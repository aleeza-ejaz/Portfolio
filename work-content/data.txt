## Overview
This semester, I completed the 25-Hour Certificate Program through New York Tech's Entrepreneurship and Technology Innovation Center [ETIC](https://www.nyit.edu/academics/engineering-and-computing-sciences/etic/), where I worked on a project exploring how AI could make large, unstructured datasets more accessible through an automade analysis pipeline which results in auto-generated data visualizations.


## Challenge
Designing an AI-powered universal schema structure that could standardize inconsistent datasets and deliver useful findings with customizable charts in one command.

### BACKGROUND 

In the Spring of 2026, I reached out to the Entrepreneurship & Innovation Technology Center (ETIC) at New York Tech in hopes of getting involved with their patent work for the NASA Technology Transfer Expansion (T2X) program. Their pre-requisite 25 Hour Certificate Program required completion of a major-aligned project to demonstrate your innovative ability and resourcefulness. 

At the same time, ETIC helped host the Whole Health Community Datathon with the NYC Civic Engagement Committee. Students worked in teams to analyze survey data collected from the five boroughs on the social determinants of health. The datasets came from the CEC's The People's Money participatory budgeting process and contained over 10,000 rows. Long survey answers often cluttered the cells, which made it hard to see relationships in the data.

The datathon centered on analysis and visualization, so I built my 25 Hour Program submission around that same problem.

## Solution
I directed the development of a Python script powered by a locally run, open-source LLM (Mistral, running through Ollama). It takes an input CSV and produces three outputs:

**1. An Excel workbook** -  The data is categorized into a 22-column universal schema. A summary tab reads the categorized data and shows counts of the different tags and the relationships found across the dataset.

**2. An interactive HTML dashboard** -  Built from the workbook, it turns the generated summaries and pre-defined relationships into customizable visualizations: bar, pie, and line charts, plus cross-tabulations (such as category by location) that can also be displayed as a heatmap. Charts are powered by Plotly.

**3. A failsafe CSV** -  An unmodified copy of the original data, kept so nothing is lost or altered during processing. 

To run it, a user installs the packages listed in a requirements file- pulling the latest Mistral model through Ollama- then runs a single command in the terminal to generate all three files.
